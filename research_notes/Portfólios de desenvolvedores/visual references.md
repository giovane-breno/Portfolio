# Developer portfolio visual references

## Comparative review

### Takeaway

The strongest references do not look “professional” because they use a fashionable stack or a dense UI kit. They feel credible because the visual system is opinionated, the interaction has a reason to exist, and the portfolio exposes proof: shipped work, specific responsibilities, public experiments, writing, talks, open source, or measurable adoption. The examples below span restrained editorial portfolios, playful personal brands, and high-interaction creative-developer sites.

### Brittany Chiang — dark editorial system with proof-rich project cards

- Site: [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com/)
- Layout/composition: a compact, single-person narrative moves from positioning and experience into selected projects and writing; the site uses short descriptions, technology labels, and a project archive rather than an undifferentiated wall of thumbnails. The current site explicitly lists roles, client work, projects, and writing. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/)
- Color/type: the current site identifies Inter as its typeface and uses a restrained technical/editorial vocabulary; the earlier v4 reference is widely recognized for a dark navy palette with a bright green accent and clear section hierarchy. — [Brittany v4](https://v4.brittanychiang.com/); [current site](https://brittanychiang.com/)
- Portraits/identity: the identity is carried primarily by concise first-person copy and links, not by a large hero portrait. That makes the work and professional record the visual center. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/)
- Project presentation: projects are framed as outcomes with context, role/stack, screenshots, and links; one example is the Halcyon theme, accompanied by an adoption signal (“100k+ Installs”), while the site itself is presented as an open-source project with 6k+ stars and 3k+ forks. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/)
- Motion restraint/credibility: the credibility comes from concrete evidence and accessibility/product language (“accessible,” “pixel-perfect,” design systems, named clients), not spectacle. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/)
- Reusable decision: use a dark neutral foundation, one memorable accent, a narrow set of featured projects, and proof-oriented metadata. Let the résumé and project evidence do more work than decorative hero effects.

### Bruno Simon — one strong metaphor, fully executed

- Site: [bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com/)
- Layout/composition: the portfolio is a navigable 3D world. Visitors drive a car through a scene to discover information, projects, and secrets; the interface exposes explicit keyboard, mouse, mobile, and gamepad controls. — [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/)
- Color/type: the visual language is a deliberately limited low-poly world: warm orange terrain/sky, a red vehicle, geometric objects, and large block lettering. A gallery capture describes the same orange landscape, red Jeep, white “BRUNO SIMON” lettering, and simple geometric forms. — [Catalin’s portfolio roundup](https://catalins.tech/14-best-web-developer-portfolios-to-get-inspiration/)
- Portraits/identity: no conventional portrait is necessary; the car/avatar and the world itself become the signature. This is appropriate because the site is selling creative development as an experience.
- Project presentation: project discovery is embedded in the world rather than arranged as a standard grid; the “behind the scene” section still provides stack, source code, devlogs, and credits, including Three.js, Blender files, and music credits. — [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/)
- Motion restraint/credibility: this is intentionally not restrained; it is a useful counterexample. The spectacle works because it is a coherent, authored metaphor, offers a low-quality mode, and documents how it was made. — [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/)
- Reusable decision: if the work is genuinely experiential, one large metaphor can replace a generic portfolio shell. Budget for onboarding, fallback controls, performance modes, and a plain-text route to contact and proof.

### Rauno Freiberg — operating-system metaphor plus sparse editorial typography

- Site: [raunofreiberg.com](https://raunofreiberg.com/)
- Layout/composition: the current portfolio is intentionally sparse: a short identity statement, a small navigation set (projects, field notes, history of software design, craft), and a memorable six-line manifesto. — [Rauno Freiberg](https://raunofreiberg.com/)
- Gallery evidence describes earlier/related Rauno portfolio treatments as an OS-like desktop with a dock, full-screen imagery, side-scrolling project/experiment feeds, and a photography grid. — [Killer Portfolio](https://www.killerportfolio.com/by/rauno-freiberg); [A1 Gallery](https://www.a1.gallery/website/rauno-freiberg)
- Color/type: design-gallery documentation identifies a light-gray/black palette, JetBrains Mono, monospaced styling, and an isometric/interactive feel; another capture of the 2025 portfolio shows black oversized text on white with a saturated yellow circular accent. — [A1 Gallery](https://www.a1.gallery/website/rauno-freiberg); [Awwwards image reference](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/portfolio-2025)
- Portraits/identity: the identity is expressed through typography, timestamps/context, interface objects, and a written philosophy rather than a standard headshot. — [Rauno Freiberg](https://raunofreiberg.com/)
- Project presentation: projects, experiments, field notes, and photography are separate destinations. That separation supports both quick scanning and deeper inspection. — [Rauno Freiberg](https://raunofreiberg.com/)
- Motion restraint/credibility: the visual novelty is concentrated in the navigation metaphor and transitions; the content model stays legible. The result feels crafted because the metaphor is consistent across navigation, imagery, and micro-interactions. — [Killer Portfolio](https://www.killerportfolio.com/by/rauno-freiberg)
- Reusable decision: pick one interface metaphor and apply it consistently to navigation, project indexing, and mode changes. Pair unusual interaction with very simple content labels.

### Lee Robinson — minimalist personal publishing system

- Site: [leerob.io](https://leerob.io/)
- Layout/composition: a small number of content areas, light background, dark text, serif-led typography, and a focused bio create a calm, editorial “home base.” A design capture reports a white/#282828 palette and only one homepage section, compared with a five-section average in its portfolio sample. — [UI Discovery](https://uidiscovery.com/sites/lee-robinson)
- Portraits/identity: earlier versions used a circular portrait and visible activity metrics; a separate visual roundup describes a concise bio plus a grid of photographs from talks, conferences, and community moments. — [Vercel portfolio template capture](https://vercel.com/new/templates/next.js/nextjs-portfolio-pageview-counter); [One Hour Digital](https://onehour.digital/developer/best-developers-portfolios-and-personal-sites)
- Project/content presentation: the current site is less a gallery than a publishing system: durable “Notes” are separated from dated blog posts, and the site itself is available as an open-source Next.js/MDX template. — [leerob.io](https://leerob.io/); [Job Roadmaps](https://jobroadmaps.com/portfolios/lee-robinson)
- Motion restraint/credibility: almost no visual noise is needed because the credibility comes from a persistent body of writing, open source, named roles, and a site structure that can survive job changes. — [Job Roadmaps](https://jobroadmaps.com/portfolios/lee-robinson)
- Reusable decision: if you have writing, documentation, or open source, make it a first-class portfolio surface. A simple site with durable content can signal more maturity than a highly animated project grid.

### Cassie Evans — personality, teaching, and motion as evidence

- Site: [cassie.codes](https://www.cassie.codes/)
- Layout/composition: the documented landing page uses a split composition: illustrated workspace on one side, bold personal statement on the other, with direct routes to writing, speaking, workshops, and play. — [WeAreDevelopers visual roundup](https://www.wearedevelopers.com/en/magazine/161/web-developer-portfolio-examples)
- Color/type: the gallery capture describes a mint/purple color system, bold typography, and a lamp illustration that doubles as a light/dark mode control. — [WeAreDevelopers](https://www.wearedevelopers.com/en/magazine/161/web-developer-portfolio-examples); [Killer Portfolio](https://www.killerportfolio.com/by/cassie-evans)
- Portraits/identity: illustration replaces a conventional portrait and communicates a playful workspace/personality. The copy is direct: making fun interactive things and talking/writing about them. — [WeAreDevelopers](https://www.wearedevelopers.com/en/magazine/161/web-developer-portfolio-examples)
- Project presentation: the strongest proof is not only a project gallery but a public body of work: CodePen demos, talks, workshops, writing, and a niche around GSAP/SVG animation. A portfolio analysis documents 100+ publicly indexed demos and live embeds. — [Killer Portfolio](https://www.killerportfolio.com/by/cassie-evans)
- Motion restraint/credibility: the useful principle is purposeful motion—animation reveals information, directs attention, or communicates personality; gratuitous motion reads as immaturity. — [Killer Portfolio](https://www.killerportfolio.com/by/cassie-evans)
- Reusable decision: make the visual personality match the specialty, and turn teaching artifacts into portfolio proof. If animation is your niche, show many small, inspectable experiments rather than one untestable splash screen.

### Lucas Vallenet — awards-backed creative developer identity

- Site: [lucasvallenet.com](https://lucasvallenet.com/)
- Layout/composition: the visible structure is deliberately simple—name, “Creative Coder” positioning, availability/contact, and an archive of work/recognition. The page does not need to explain every project before visitors can see the breadth of practice. — [Lucas Vallenet](https://lucasvallenet.com/)
- Color/type and imagery: the page’s strongest visual device is the archive itself: a long, chronological record of awarded work. This creates a portfolio-as-index feel rather than a conventional résumé. — [Lucas Vallenet](https://lucasvallenet.com/)
- Portraits/identity: personal credibility is built through a clear role (“Creative Coder”), current availability, and a consistent name attached to the work; the site does not depend on a large portrait.
- Project presentation: the archive lists individual projects with award bodies and categories. The site records repeated recognitions from Awwwards, CSSDA, FWA, and others across 2015–2025, including developer, site-of-the-day, typography, and mobile awards. — [Lucas Vallenet](https://lucasvallenet.com/)
- Motion restraint/credibility: the credibility is external and cumulative—recognition, chronology, and named work—rather than a claim that the portfolio itself is “award-winning.” — [Lucas Vallenet](https://lucasvallenet.com/)
- Reusable decision: when your work has meaningful external validation, show it as an organized archive with dates and categories. Awards should annotate work, not replace the work.

### Josh Comeau — editorial learning product with playful illustration

- Site/reference: [joshwcomeau.com](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/); [portfolio guidance](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Layout/composition: the visual reference is a dark editorial layout with clear navigation, article lists, category browsing, and a small playful 3D character/illustration. External captures describe a dark navy background, white text, pink headings, a two-column content/sidebar structure, and prominent recent/popular content. — [Landing Love capture](https://www.landing.love/sites/joshwcomeau/); [Scrimba capture](https://scrimba.com/articles/minimal-web-developer-portfolio-examples/)
- Portraits/identity: a stylized character and a distinctive teaching voice substitute for a formal headshot. The identity is “the person who makes difficult frontend ideas intuitive,” reinforced by interactive explanations and tutorials. — [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Project/content presentation: the site behaves like a product/content system, not a résumé. Comeau’s own portfolio guidance recommends curating roughly 2–5 projects, guiding readers through an under-the-hood exploration, and writing personal, memorable copy. — [Build a portfolio that appeals to employers](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Motion restraint/credibility: interactivity is used to teach and demonstrate concepts, not only to decorate the home page. The credibility comes from depth, clarity, and a long public record of tutorials. — [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Reusable decision: design the portfolio around the behavior you want to be hired for. If you teach, make the site teach; if you build products, make the project pages feel like product documentation.

### Gallery pattern — Valentin Gassend and current creative-developer references

- Reference gallery: [CSS Nectar portfolio nominees](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery/nominees/categories/portfolio/)
- The gallery’s Valentin Gassend entry describes a portfolio that combines in-depth case studies with a dedicated experimental “Lab,” using WebGL shaders, kinetic typography, and smooth scrolling. — [CSS Nectar](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery/nominees/categories/portfolio/)
- The same gallery describes a more restrained Berke Sasa portfolio: minimal design, clean typography, a custom intro animation, and shared-layout transitions while keeping content primary. — [CSS Nectar](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery/nominees/categories/portfolio/)
- Reusable decision: creative portfolios can separate “proof of shipped work” from “proof of technical curiosity.” Put case studies in a stable, readable structure and isolate experiments in a Lab so the experimental layer does not obscure the professional one.

## Reusable design decisions

### Takeaway

To avoid a generic AI/vibecode look, the portfolio should make fewer, more deliberate promises. The most transferable system is a restrained editorial base with one authored point of view, a small number of high-quality case studies, and visible proof of how the developer thinks and ships.

### Cited Findings

- Curate rather than enumerate: Josh Comeau recommends roughly 2–5 projects and emphasizes guiding readers through the work’s interesting decisions. — [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Make the first screen legible in seconds: Brittany Chiang and Lee Robinson both establish role/identity early, then let projects or writing carry the depth. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/); [Lee Robinson](https://leerob.io/)
- Use a small, intentional palette: the references repeatedly rely on a neutral base plus one strong identity color—Brittany’s dark/accent treatment, Rauno’s black/white/yellow treatment, Cassie’s bright split colors, and Josh’s navy/pink/illustration system. — [Brittany Chiang](https://v4.brittanychiang.com/); [Awwwards Rauno reference](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/portfolio-2025); [WeAreDevelopers Cassie reference](https://www.wearedevelopers.com/en/magazine/161/web-developer-portfolio-examples); [Landing Love Josh reference](https://www.landing.love/sites/joshwcomeau/)
- Use portraits selectively: portraits are not mandatory. Several strong examples use illustration, avatar, interface metaphor, or work imagery; when a portrait is used, it should support trust and context rather than act as a decorative hero asset. — [Lee Robinson visual capture](https://vercel.com/new/templates/next.js/nextjs-portfolio-pageview-counter); [Josh Comeau about page](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/about-josh/); [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/)
- Treat motion as a communication tool: Cassie’s documented principle is the clearest rule—motion should reveal, direct, or communicate personality. Berke Sasa is described as using a restrained intro animation and shared-layout transitions while keeping content primary. — [Cassie Evans analysis](https://jobroadmaps.com/portfolios/cassie-evans); [CSS Nectar Berke Sasa](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery/nominees/categories/portfolio/)
- Put experiments behind an explicit boundary: Rauno’s projects/field notes and CSS Nectar’s “Lab” pattern show how to preserve a credible core while still exposing technical curiosity. — [Rauno Freiberg](https://raunofreiberg.com/); [CSS Nectar](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery/nominees/categories/portfolio/)
- Show proof in the same visual system: adoption numbers, named clients, open-source links, writing, talks, devlogs, awards, and source code are more credible when attached to the relevant project instead of placed in a generic “skills” cloud. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/); [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/); [Cassie Evans](https://www.cassie.codes/); [Lucas Vallenet](https://lucasvallenet.com/)

### Inferences

- A credible developer portfolio should have a “quiet mode”: readable typography, direct navigation, visible contact, and project summaries that work even if all animation is disabled. This is inferred from the contrast between spectacle-heavy Bruno Simon and the consistently legible content models in Brittany Chiang, Lee Robinson, and Josh Comeau. — [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/); [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/); [Lee Robinson](https://leerob.io/); [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Distinctiveness is more credible when it is earned by the person’s actual specialty. Bruno’s world, Cassie’s motion work, Rauno’s interface craft, and Josh’s interactive teaching all make the site itself evidence of the work they claim to do. — [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/); [Cassie Evans](https://jobroadmaps.com/portfolios/cassie-evans); [Rauno Freiberg](https://raunofreiberg.com/); [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- The safest visual recipe for a generalist developer is not “more effects”; it is one strong editorial composition, one distinctive type/color decision, and project pages with real decisions, constraints, screenshots, role, and outcome. This is an inference from the higher proof density of the restrained examples. — [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/); [Lee Robinson](https://leerob.io/); [Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/)
- Animation restraint is partly an information-architecture problem: if the portfolio has clear destinations for work, writing, experiments, and contact, animation can stay local and purposeful instead of carrying the burden of making the whole site feel “interesting.” — [Rauno Freiberg](https://raunofreiberg.com/); [Cassie Evans](https://jobroadmaps.com/portfolios/cassie-evans); [CSS Nectar](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery/nominees/categories/portfolio/)

### Gaps

- I could not reliably inspect every live portfolio’s full responsive/mobile behavior from text search alone; gallery screenshots and indexed descriptions are strongest for initial composition, not exhaustive interaction QA.
- Award/gallery descriptions are useful secondary evidence of visual treatment but can be promotional or incomplete. For implementation decisions, verify each live site directly and test keyboard navigation, reduced motion, loading performance, and mobile fallback.
- Some portfolios change over time. This note captures the accessible pages and indexed captures available during the research pass; older Brittany and Lee visual references are explicitly labeled as earlier versions/captures where applicable.
